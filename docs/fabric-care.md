# Fabric and care

Looking after your clothes properly keeps them looking good for years. This page covers washing, drying and ironing for the fabrics we use. Always check the care label sewn into each garment first, as it overrides the general advice here.

## General rules

- Wash similar colors together and turn garments inside out.
- Use a mild detergent and avoid bleach.
- Close zippers and buttons before washing.
- Do not leave wet clothes in the machine, as this can cause creases and odors.
- Dry flat or on a hanger away from direct sunlight, which can fade dyes.

## Cotton

Machine wash cold on a gentle cycle. Cotton may shrink slightly the first time it is washed, usually by less than 3 percent, so avoid hot water. Tumble dry on low or line dry. Iron on a medium setting while the fabric is slightly damp.

## Linen

Machine wash cold on a gentle cycle, and avoid overloading the machine. Linen softens with every wash and is meant to look relaxed. Line dry, or remove from the dryer while slightly damp to avoid hard creases. Iron on a high setting with steam, or leave it unironed for a lived-in look.

## Wool blends and knitwear

Hand wash in cool water with a wool-safe detergent, or use the wool cycle with a mesh bag. Do not wring or twist. Press out water with a towel and dry flat, reshaping the garment while damp. Never hang knitwear to dry, as the weight stretches it. Store folded instead of on a hanger. Fuzzy balls of fiber called pilling are normal and can be removed with a fabric shaver.

## Denim

Turn jeans and denim jackets inside out. Wash less often than other clothes, as every wash fades the color. Use cold water, a gentle cycle and a little detergent. Hang to dry.

### Color bleeding on the first wash

New dark and indigo denim, and some deeply dyed cotton, can release extra dye the first time it is washed. This is normal, and it is not a fault. To protect your other clothes and the garment itself:

- Wash the item on its own for the first wash, and the next one or two washes if the water is still colored.
- Use cold water only.
- Do not soak it for long.
- Never wash it with white or light-colored items until the water runs clear.

Dye transfer can also rub off on light furniture and bags when the denim is new, so take care for the first few wears.

## Stains

Treat stains quickly. Blot, do not rub, and rinse from the back of the fabric with cold water. Avoid hot water, which can set stains. For oil stains, apply a little mild dish soap before washing. Test any stain treatment on a hidden area first.

## Dry cleaning

Garments marked "dry clean only" should not be washed at home. Structured jackets and some wool pieces fall into this group.

## Storage

Keep clothes in a cool, dry place. Fold knitwear and hang dresses, jackets and pants on wide hangers. Use breathable garment bags rather than plastic for long-term storage.

## Damage and wear

Normal wear, including fading, pilling and shrinkage that follows the care label, is not treated as a defect. If an item arrived with a fault, see the damaged or wrong item page, as there is a time limit for reporting.
